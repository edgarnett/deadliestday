The deadliest day of the year for child pedestrians.

Source: NHTSA Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS), National CSV files 2000–2024 (2025 not yet released). Deaths are people with injury severity "fatal" (died within 30 days), by crash date. Pedestrians are person type 5; bicyclists include other cyclists; motorcyclists are drivers and passengers of motorcycles (vehicle body types 80–89); "other" includes occupants of parked vehicles and unknown road users; children are under 18, and unknown ages are not counted as children. "All road deaths" means every person killed in a crash on a public road, whether a driver, passenger, pedestrian or cyclist. "Evening" is 6 pm to midnight by crash hour. Map locations come from the crash report's latitude and longitude; deaths without usable coordinates, and those in Alaska and Hawaii, are not drawn. Totals are small for single weekdays (3–4 Halloweens each), so those comparisons are suggestive, not conclusive. The roughly 3× child-pedestrian risk is consistent with a 2019 JAMA Pediatrics study. Code and data: github.com/edgarnett/deadliestday.
